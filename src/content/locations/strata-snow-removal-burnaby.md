## Section 31 is stricter than most councils realise

Most Metro Vancouver sidewalk bylaws give property owners until 10am and quietly stand down
on statutory holidays. Burnaby's does not. Section 31 of the Street and Traffic Bylaw makes
the owner or occupier responsible for clearing the sidewalks around the property **by 10am
every day, holidays included**, to a minimum cleared width of 1.2 metres — and the fine for
a business runs at $80 per day.

For a strata corporation that duty sits with council. It is not the city's job and it is not
the individual owner's job, and a corporation that assumes otherwise usually finds out on
the morning of 26 December.

The 1.2 metre figure matters more than it looks. It is a width, not a path — a shovel-width
track down the middle of a sidewalk is not compliance, and it is also the exact thing a
plaintiff's photograph will show three inches of.

## Two winters in one city

Burnaby is the only municipality we serve where the same contract can span a genuine climate
split. Big Bend sits close to sea level on the Fraser flats. Burnaby Mountain runs to roughly
370 metres. In a marginal storm — which is most of them here — the lower property is getting
rain while the upper one is accumulating, and a contractor running one trigger for both is
either over-servicing the bottom or missing the top.

We treat elevation as a routing input, not a footnote. A Metrotown or Brentwood tower, a Big
Bend townhouse complex and an SFU-area property sit on different trigger thresholds in the
same plan, which is also why a single flat per-visit price across a mixed portfolio tends to
be wrong in both directions.

## Where Burnaby strata properties actually fail

The recurring problem is not the parking lot. It is the seam between surfaces: the lip where
a parkade ramp meets the sidewalk, the transition from a cleared drive aisle onto an
uncleared visitor path, the shaded north side of a tower that refreezes after everything
else has dried.

Parkade ramps are the sharpest case. A ramp that drains toward the street sends meltwater
across the bylaw frontage, where it refreezes overnight into exactly the surface Section 31
exists to prevent. Treating the ramp and ignoring the ten feet below it produces a compliant
ramp and a liability.

## What council actually needs from a contractor

A strata council is a volunteer body making a decision it may have to defend to owners at an
AGM, and occasionally to an insurer. That changes what a good proposal looks like:

- **A stated response window**, in hours, written into the agreement rather than implied by
  "24/7 monitoring".
- **Bylaw frontage mapped in**, so compliance is part of the service rather than a separate
  conversation each storm.
- **A record per visit** that exists whether or not anyone asks for it — because the request
  usually comes months later, from a lawyer.
- **A price that does not move.** Salt is inside our rate rather than added to the invoice
  afterwards, which is the most common source of a February surprise in this trade.
