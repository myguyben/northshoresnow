## The real deadline is your first arrival, not your opening time

Almost every commercial snow conversation is framed around opening hours. It is the wrong
frame. The person genuinely at risk is whoever unlocks the building — the manager in at
5:30, the kitchen staff, the warehouse supervisor — crossing an untreated lot in the dark,
alone, an hour or more before anyone else.

That is why our core North Vancouver routes are cleared **before 6:30am**, with full North
Shore coverage by 8am, and why those times are written into the agreement rather than
implied. As far as we can establish, no other contractor in this market publishes a response
time at all.

## North Vancouver's commercial geography is three different problems

**Marine Drive and the Lonsdale corridor** are frontage problems. Long, public, continuous
sidewalk along a busy street, in front of businesses whose customers judge them on it. It is
also the surface most likely to be treated as the city's job. It is not — the owner or
occupier is responsible for the sidewalk bordering the property, and in the District,
commercial property does not get the 10am grace period detached homes are given.

**Harbourside, Maplewood and the Dollarton corridor** are exposure problems. These sites sit
on open ground near the water with little shelter. Wind redistributes whatever falls and
packs it into the lee of buildings, along fence lines and against dock doors — which stays
long after the open yard has cleared and is denser than anything that fell there.

**Upper Lonsdale, Edgemont, Lynn Valley and the Capilano bench** are elevation problems. A
business at 200 metres services roughly twice as often across a season as one near the
water. Same city, same contractor, genuinely different winter.

## What that means for a quote

A flat per-visit rate across a North Shore portfolio is wrong at one end of the hill. We
measure the actual serviceable area — the drive lanes, the walkways, the loading approach,
the frontage — and price it against the frequency your elevation implies.

The number is all-in. De-icer is inside the rate rather than added to the invoice afterwards,
which matters here because a hillside site needs material on most visits and an à-la-carte
material charge is the most common reason a season comes in over budget.

Snow clearing and de-icing are still separate services at separate rates, because a cold
clear morning needs salt and no plough, and you should not be billed as though it needed
both.
