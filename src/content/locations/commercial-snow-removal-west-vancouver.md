## Two commercial West Vancouvers

The District's commercial property splits cleanly, and the two halves want different things
from a snow contractor.

**The village strips** — Ambleside and Dundarave along Marine Drive — are frontage-first.
Small retail units, professional offices and clinics with a handful of parking stalls each and
a long shared sidewalk out front. Here the sidewalk is not a compliance obligation so much as
the shop window: it is where a customer decides whether you are open and whether the walk to
your door is worth it.

Neighbouring frontages tend to be cleared inconsistently, which cuts both ways. A cleared
strip in front of an otherwise patchy block is conspicuous — and so is the reverse.

**The hillside offices and the Park Royal periphery** are access-first. Grade, exposure and
larger lots, where the question is whether staff and clients can get in and out at all.

## The 24-hour rule is misunderstood by almost everyone

West Vancouver requires the sidewalk bordering a property to be cleared **within 24 hours of
a snowfall**, and separately prohibits shovelling snow onto the roadway.

Compared with Burnaby and Richmond — both 10am, every day — that is generous, and businesses
read it as permission to wait. It is not. Twenty-four hours is when the District can act. It
has no bearing on when your customers arrive, when your staff arrive, or when someone slips
on your landing. A business that clears on hour twenty-three is compliant and has had a bad
day.

## Nowhere to put it, and the road is out

A village-strip business typically has six to twelve stalls and no landscaped margin. The
default answer — push it to the street — is explicitly prohibited here.

What is left is a planned storage location that does not eat a stall, block a sightline at a
crosswalk, or bury the catch basin that the next thaw needs. On genuinely constrained
properties that means hauling, which we quote up front rather than introducing after a heavy
week.

## The early-morning problem

Marine Drive carries an unusual concentration of medical and professional offices, and a
meaningful share of their patients are elderly. An icy entry landing at a clinic is not the
same risk as an icy corner of a retail lot.

Entry landings, the accessible approach and the path from the accessible stall are mapped as
priority hand-work surfaces and treated on the same visit, not added on a second pass. Our
core routes are cleared before 6:30am, ahead of most clinic openings — and that window is in
the agreement rather than implied.
