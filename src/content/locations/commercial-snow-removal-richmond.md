## In Richmond, the de-icer does more work than the plough

Most commercial snow contracts are written around accumulation: how deep before we come, how
often, how much. In Richmond that framing quietly misses the actual risk.

This is flat delta ground, most of it at or just above sea level, sitting between the Fraser
and the strait. Three things follow, and all three matter more than snowfall totals:

**Meltwater stands.** A large lot cleared at 5am holds water in its low bays through the day
and refreezes it overnight. The resulting sheet is clear, invisible in headlights and
covering a surface that looks properly serviced.

**Freezing fog does not need a storm.** Moisture off the river and the strait, on a still
night, glazes asphalt and concrete with no precipitation at all. A contract triggered only by
snowfall never dispatches on those nights — and those are the nights people fall.

**Wind off open ground.** There is very little between a Richmond industrial site and the
water. Drifting redistributes what has fallen and packs it into the shaded lee of buildings
and fences, where it stays long after the open lot has dried.

## The three commercial Richmonds

**Big-box retail** — Bridgeport, Lansdowne, the Ackroyd and Alderbridge corridors. Enormous
flat lots where the constraint is drainage rather than space. These are the sites where
anti-icing before a freeze is worth more than a plough after a snowfall, because once a
five-acre lot has frozen over you are treating a rink, not a parking lot.

**Warehouse and distribution** — Bridgeport, Ironwood, the Mitchell Island and Fraserport
areas. Yards that run around the clock, so there is no empty window. Clearing gets sequenced
against live dock activity: approach, dock aprons and turning area first, general yard behind
it.

**YVR-area industrial and airport support** — sites where the operation genuinely does not
stop and where a shift change at 6am is the moment the surface has to be right.

## Sidewalk frontage, Sundays included

Bylaw 5870 requires owners and occupiers of residential, commercial and industrial property
to clear snow and ice from the sidewalk along the property line **by 10:00am every day,
Sundays included**, with a $125 fine for missing it.

Commercial frontages in Richmond are often long — a big-box parcel or a warehouse lot can
present a couple of hundred metres to the street — and it is the surface most likely to be
treated as somebody else's problem until a complaint arrives.

## What to ask a contractor bidding Richmond work

Anyone can plough a flat lot. The questions that separate contractors here are narrower:

- **Does de-icing dispatch on temperature, independent of snowfall?** If not, the contract
  will sit idle through the mornings that actually matter.
- **Where does the meltwater go, and what is the plan when it refreezes?**
- **How is the yard sequenced against live dock traffic?**
- **What is the response window in hours?** Nobody in this market publishes one. Ours is in
  the agreement.
- **Is material inside the price or added to the invoice?** Ours is inside it — which in a
  city where most visits are de-icing visits is the single biggest variable in the season
  total.
