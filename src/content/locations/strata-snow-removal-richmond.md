## Richmond's winter problem is drainage, not depth

Richmond does not get the accumulation the North Shore does. What it gets is worse in a
specific way: **water with nowhere to go.**

The city sits on flat delta ground, most of it at or barely above sea level. On a sloped
property, meltwater from a cleared surface runs off. Here it does not. It stands where it
melted — in the low spot of a drive aisle, at the foot of an entry step, across the width of
a visitor stall — and when the temperature drops through zero that night, it becomes a sheet
of clear ice over a surface that looks cleared.

That is the Richmond hazard: not a snowbank someone can see, but a transparent sheet on a
surface that was properly ploughed eight hours earlier.

## Ice with no snow at all

The second thing that catches Richmond strata out is that the worst mornings often follow
nights with no precipitation.

Freezing fog rolls off the Fraser and the strait. Clear, still nights radiate heat away from
paved surfaces until they sit below the air temperature. Either produces a glaze on walkways
and drive aisles without a flake falling — and a contract that dispatches on snowfall
accumulation simply does not send anyone.

This is why we treat de-icing as a separate service with its own trigger and its own rate
rather than as something bundled into a plough visit. In a Richmond winter, most of the
billable visits are de-icing. Some seasons nearly all of them are.

## Bylaw 5870 includes Sundays

Richmond's Traffic Control and Regulation Bylaw 5870 requires the owner or occupier of
residential, commercial and industrial property to clear snow and ice from the sidewalk along
the property line **by 10:00am every day, Sundays included**. The fine is $125.

For a strata corporation the duty runs to council. The frontage is often long on a Richmond
townhouse development — these complexes tend to present a wide face to the street — and it is
the one surface that is simultaneously the corporation's responsibility and entirely outside
its fence line.

## Almost everything outside is common property

Richmond's strata stock leans heavily toward low-rise townhouse developments rather than
towers, and that changes the scope. In a tower, the corporation's outdoor obligation is often
a lobby approach and a ramp. In a townhouse complex it is the internal drive aisles, the
visitor parking, the walkways between buildings, the entry steps to every unit, and the
bylaw frontage.

That is a lot of surface, most of it hand-work rather than machine-work, and it is the reason
a price quoted off a satellite outline of the whole parcel is usually wrong. We measure the
surfaces actually serviced and price those.
