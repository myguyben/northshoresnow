## The deadline is 10am, and it does not take holidays off

Section 31 of Burnaby's Street and Traffic Bylaw puts the duty on the owner or occupier of
the property: sidewalks around the property cleared **by 10am every day, including
holidays**, to a minimum cleared width of 1.2 metres. For a business the fine is $80 per day.

Two details catch commercial operators out. The first is that holidays are included — most
neighbouring bylaws are read as standing down on statutory days, and Burnaby's is not. The
second is the width. A shovelled track is not a cleared sidewalk, and 1.2 metres is also
roughly what an accessibility complaint or a slip-and-fall photograph will be measured
against.

## Three Burnabys, three problems

**Big Bend** is industrial and flat, close to the Fraser and close to sea level. Its yards
are dominated by manoeuvring space rather than parking rows, which changes where snow can go:
push it to the convenient edge and you have taken away the swing a 53-foot trailer needs to
square up on a dock. Yard clearing here is a geometry problem before it is a volume problem.

**Metrotown and Brentwood** are the opposite — dense retail and mixed-use where the
constraint is time, not space. These lots fill continuously from opening, and a lot that is
not cleared before the doors open does not get properly cleared that day. It gets worked
around, one aisle at a time, while customers park in the windrows.

**Burnaby Mountain and the upper slopes** run roughly 370 metres above the Fraser flats. In
a marginal storm the lower city gets rain and the upper city accumulates. A contractor
running a single trigger depth across a Burnaby portfolio is guessing at one end of it.

## Snow storage is a design decision

Burnaby's older commercial strips — stretches of Kingsway, Hastings, Edmonds — were built
building-to-sidewalk with no landscaped margin to lose. There is no natural place to put
snow, which means either a planned storage location that does not block sightlines, drainage
or accessible parking, or hauling.

Both are fine. What is not fine is discovering the problem during the storm, which is how
snow ends up piled against a fire hydrant, over a catch basin, or in the accessible stall
because it was the only space nobody was using at 4am.

We agree storage before the season and quote hauling up front where the site needs it.
