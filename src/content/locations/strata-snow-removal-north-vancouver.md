## The District holds multi-family to a stricter standard

This surprises most councils, so it is worth stating plainly. In the District of North
Vancouver, **detached homes have until 10am** to clear the sidewalk bordering their property.
**Commercial, industrial and multi-family property is not given that window** — the
expectation is prompt clearing after a snowfall.

A townhouse complex is therefore held to a tighter standard than the single-family house
across the street, and a council operating on the 10am figure it read somewhere is working
to the wrong rule.

## The hill is the job

North Vancouver runs from sea level to around 300 metres over a few kilometres, and strata
properties are scattered the whole way up. That produces two problems no flat municipality
has.

**The first is frequency.** A Lower Lonsdale building and a Braemar or Upper Delbrook
complex do not experience the same winter. In a marginal storm the lower property gets rain
and the upper one accumulates. Across a season the difference is not marginal — the hillside
property can trigger roughly twice as often. A contractor quoting a flat per-visit rate
across a North Shore portfolio has mispriced at least one end of it.

**The second is the shared driveway.** Most hillside strata here are organised around a
single steep access drive with parking off it. That drive is not one surface among many; it
is the surface. If it is not passable nobody leaves, and partial clearing achieves nothing —
a car cannot use half a slope.

A steep drive also cannot be cleared and left. Bare wet asphalt on a grade refreezes into
something considerably more dangerous than the packed snow that was there before, which is
why material goes down on the same visit rather than on a return trip.

## Where North Vancouver strata properties actually fail

- **Entry steps and landings.** Hand-work, north-facing, usually shaded, and the last thing
  to get attention. Also where residents actually fall.
- **The transition from drive to walkway.** Machine-cleared to hand-cleared is a seam, and
  seams refreeze.
- **Visitor parking.** Nobody clears it early because nobody is in it early, and then it
  fills at the worst possible time.
- **The bylaw frontage**, which is the corporation's responsibility and sits outside its
  fence.

## What council should be able to see

A strata council is a volunteer body that may have to justify the award to owners at an AGM
and, occasionally, to an insurer after an incident. The things worth insisting on:

- **A response window in hours**, written into the agreement. On the North Shore ours are
  published: core routes before 6:30am, full North Shore coverage by 8am.
- **Elevation reflected in the plan**, not a single trigger depth applied to a mountain.
- **A record per visit** that exists whether or not anyone asks — because the request comes
  months later.
- **Salt inside the rate**, not added afterwards. On a hillside property that needs material
  on most visits, an à-la-carte charge is where the budget goes.
