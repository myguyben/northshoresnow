## Downtown is a treatment problem, not a ploughing problem

Almost everything written about commercial snow removal assumes a parking lot. A downtown
strata tower does not have one.

What it has is a set of narrow, high-consequence surfaces: the sidewalk frontage on every
street the building touches, the parkade ramp, the lobby approach and entry landings, the
loading bay apron, and sometimes a plaza or courtyard the corporation owns and the public
walks across.

Nearly all of it is hand-work and de-icer rather than machine clearing. That matters for
pricing, because an area-based quote derived from the outline of a tower's footprint
describes almost none of the actual work.

## The parkade ramp is where downtown buildings fail

The ramp is the single most reliable ice hazard on a downtown strata property, and the
mechanism is structural rather than a lapse.

A parkade is warmer than the street. Snow tracked in melts, water runs down the slope, and it
reaches the coldest point — the lip where the ramp meets the sidewalk — at the moment the
overnight temperature bottoms out. It refreezes there, in a strip a metre or two wide, across
the full width of the only vehicle access to the building.

Treating the ramp and stopping at the property line produces a clean ramp and an ice band at
the bottom of it. The ramp and its lip are one surface and have to be serviced as one.

## There is genuinely nowhere to put snow

A downtown tower occupies its parcel to the property line. No boulevard, no landscaped
margin, no unused corner. The places snow gets put by default are all wrong: against a
building entrance, over a catch basin the thaw will need, in the loading zone, or on the
sidewalk the City requires cleared by 10am.

So for most downtown buildings the honest answer is that snow leaves the site. We establish
before the season whether any on-site staging is possible at all, and where it is not,
removal is quoted up front. A pile left on a downtown frontage generates a complaint the same
day.

## Two or three frontages, all yours

The City of Vancouver requires property owners to clear the sidewalks bordering their
property **by 10am after a snowfall**. For a corner tower that is two frontages; for a
through-block site it can be three.

Downtown frontages are also long, continuously used, and watched. This is the surface where
a strata corporation is most likely to be photographed, reported, or named in a complaint —
and it is entirely outside the building envelope, which is exactly why it gets forgotten.
