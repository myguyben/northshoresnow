## Twenty-four hours is a deadline, not a standard of care

West Vancouver gives property owners **24 hours after a snowfall** to clear the sidewalk
bordering the property, and separately prohibits shovelling snow onto the roadway. By Metro
Vancouver standards that is a generous window — Burnaby and Richmond both demand 10am, every
day.

It is also the single most misread fact in West Vancouver strata management, because a
council reads "24 hours" and concludes there is no urgency.

The bylaw deadline and the corporation's exposure are two different things. Occupiers'
liability does not pause for a day. Residents walk out at 7am on the morning of the snowfall,
not at 7am the following day, and if someone falls then, "we were inside the bylaw window" is
not the defence it sounds like. The deadline sets when the District can fine you. It does not
set when your residents start walking.

## The Properties are a gradient

Above the highway, West Vancouver is organised around sustained slope. Complexes in the
British Properties, Chartwell, Westmount and the Chelsea Park benches sit on roads that climb
continuously, and that changes the work in a way flat-ground contractors consistently
underestimate.

On grade, **clearing alone makes the surface worse.** Ploughed asphalt on a slope holds a
film of meltwater which refreezes overnight into polished ice — harder to walk on and far
harder to drive on than the packed snow that was there at 5am. The access road has to be
treated on the same visit, not on a follow-up.

The access road is also the priority surface, full stop. Nothing else on a hillside property
matters if residents cannot get down the hill.

## Snow has nowhere obvious to go

The rule against shovelling snow onto the roadway removes the default answer, and West
Vancouver's geography removes most of the alternatives. The benched streets above Marine
Drive are narrow, often without a boulevard, frequently with a retaining wall on one side and
a drop on the other.

So storage is decided before the season: where it can go without blocking a sightline at a
blind corner, without burying a catch basin that the next thaw will need, and without
occupying the visitor stalls. On properties that genuinely have nowhere, hauling is quoted
up front rather than raised as an extra after the third storm.

## Ambleside and Dundarave are a different property entirely

Down at the water the problem inverts. Village-strip complexes and the mixed-use buildings
along Marine Drive have very little grade and a great deal of frontage — long, continuous,
highly visible sidewalk in front of shops and offices, plus the pedestrian traffic that goes
with them.

Here the failure is rarely the parking. It is the frontage, the entry landings and the
shaded north side of a building that stays frozen for a day after everything else has dried.
